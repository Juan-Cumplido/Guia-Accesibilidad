Read this first: the design barrier

A visually polished prototype can still conceal an illogical reading order, missing focus and error states, poor contrast, cognitive overload, or interactions that require a mouse. Make these decisions visible and test them before coding.

What to do

Integrate accessibility during design

  1. Prototype complete critical flows, including focus, errors, loading, responsive reflow, and assistive-technology annotations.
  2. Use accessible patterns, design systems, and reusable component specifications.
  3. Review and iterate designs with accessibility specialists and people with disabilities.

Learning path: begin with Foundation cards, continue with Intermediate resources, and use Advanced resources in semesters 6–8 or with specialist guidance.

Progressive learning

Choose a depth for your semester

The resource level is guidance, not a restriction. A student can move forward when they can explain the user impact and verify the result.

  1. Semester 2Follow Foundation procedures step by step and complete the provided comparison and checklist.
  2. Semester 4Create Foundation evidence independently and begin Intermediate resources with teacher feedback.
  3. Semester 6Connect Intermediate work across phases and perform selected Advanced activities with supervision.
  4. Semester 8Lead Advanced work, justify method choices, involve appropriate stakeholders, and document limitations.
