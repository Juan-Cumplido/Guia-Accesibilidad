How to use the guide and choose your level

Choose a development phase or browse the artifact and practice catalogs. Each resource includes steps, examples, acceptance criteria, and WCAG references. Use the level filter to match the work to your current experience.

Basic

Semesters 2–4: create accessible user stories, identify labels, and follow guided checklists. In semester 2, advanced accessibility tasks may be difficult because they require programming, research, and testing experience.

Intermediate

Semesters 4–6: connect requirements, design, code, and tests; review prototypes and document evidence with increasing independence. In semester 4, begin with teacher feedback.

Advanced

Semesters 6–9: work with automation, formal models, audits, and evaluation with users. In semester 6, use supervision. By semesters 8 or 9, you should be prepared to work across all three levels, requesting specialist support when needed.

The semester recommendations are guidance, not a guarantee of competence or a restriction. Move forward when you understand the prerequisites, can explain the user impact, and can verify the result.
