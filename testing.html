Testing

Read this first: the testing barrier

Automated tools detect only a subset of WCAG. A high score can coexist with keyboard traps, confusing announcements, inaccessible tasks, or barriers experienced by real users. Testing must combine complementary evidence.

What to do

Integrate accessibility during testing

  1. Run automated checks continuously for reliably detectable failures.
  2. Inspect keyboard, focus, zoom, reflow, semantics, errors, and assistive-technology behavior manually.
  3. Evaluate critical tasks with representative users and combine conformance findings with task outcomes.

Learning path: begin with Foundation cards, continue with Intermediate resources, and use Advanced resources in semesters 6–8 or with specialist guidance.

Progressive learning

Choose a depth for your semester

The resource level is guidance, not a restriction. A student can move forward when they can explain the user impact and verify the result.

  1. Semester 2Follow Foundation procedures step by step and complete the provided comparison and checklist.
  2. Semester 4Create Foundation evidence independently and begin Intermediate resources with teacher feedback.
  3. Semester 6Connect Intermediate work across phases and perform selected Advanced activities with supervision.
  4. Semester 8Lead Advanced work, justify method choices, involve appropriate stakeholders, and document limitations.

Combine qualitative and quantitative evidence

No single branch can demonstrate accessibility. Plan at least one method from each side when project scope and ethics allow it, and always explain remaining coverage gaps.

Qualitative branch 1

Expert and manual review

Use WCAG checklists, keyboard and focus inspection, screen readers, heuristic review, and Barrier Walkthroughs to explain why a barrier occurs and how severe its impact is.

Qualitative branch 2

Evaluation with users

Use observation, interviews, focus groups, think-aloud, and accessible co-evaluation with people with disabilities. Do not generalize a small sample to every user.

Quantitative branch 1

Conformance and technical measures

Count confirmed WCAG violations by criterion and severity, track coverage, and use tool scores only as a trend indicator—not as proof of conformance.

Quantitative branch 2

Task performance measures

Measure task completion, time, errors, assistance, and workload or satisfaction scales. Interpret numbers together with observations and participant context.

Recommendation: triangulate automation, manual or expert review, assistive technology, and user evidence before a release decision.
